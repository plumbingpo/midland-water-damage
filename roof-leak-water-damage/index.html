Roof Leak Water Damage in Midland: What to Know

The spot on your ceiling is seldom directly below the hole in the roof. Rain slips under a lifted shingle, a cracked flashing joint or a tired skylight seal, then runs along the decking and rafters before it drops onto the insulation. By the time a brown ring shows on the ceiling, the attic above it has often been wet for a while. Our team maps everything that got wet, then dries it.

We are a water damage crew, not a roofing company. We work on the interior, which means removing wet insulation, drying drywall and framing, dealing with mold and rebuilding the ceiling. If rain is still coming in, we can tarp the roof as a temporary cover, or work alongside the roofer you choose so the leak is fixed before we close anything up.

First Five Minutes

Your First Steps

  1. Cut Power to Wet FixturesIf water is dripping through a light fixture, a ceiling fan or near an outlet, switch off that circuit at the breaker and leave the fixture alone.
  2. Contain the WaterPut a pan or bucket beneath each drip, and shift rugs, electronics and furniture clear of the wet area. Protect the floor with plastic sheeting or old towels.
  3. Relieve a Ceiling BulgeIf the ceiling is holding a pocket of water, stand to one side and poke a small hole at the lowest point so it drains into a bucket.
  4. Stay Off the RoofClimbing on slick shingles or crawling around a dark attic is not worth the risk. Just observe the entry point from the room below, then hand the job to a trained crew.
  5. Photograph the DamageUse your phone to record the damaged ceiling, attic and belongings first, hold off on throwing things out until that is done, and call us to get the drying started.
The Usual Culprits

How It Usually Starts

  • Wind-torn, curling or split shingles that leave the underlayment open to rain.
  • Roof valleys where worn metal or piled debris lets water get under the covering.
  • Nails that have backed out of the decking and left small holes through the shingles.
  • Gutters packed with leaves, which push water back beneath the first row of shingles.
  • Holes and fractures left in the roof after a branch or hailstones hit it.
What Is at Risk

Where the Damage Spreads

Insulation in the Attic

Once soaked, insulation flattens, loses its value and keeps the drywall under it damp for days. In some cases fiberglass batts dry out and stay. Removal is the usual answer for wet cellulose.

Inside the Walls

Water that reaches the top of a wall runs down inside it, wetting insulation and the back of the drywall. You might notice nothing more than a peeling patch or a puffy baseboard.

Mold in the Attic

A dark, warm attic with damp wood and paper-faced drywall suits mold well. It may take hold in 24 to 48 hours and work its way over the underside of the sheathing.

Lights and Wiring

Because fixtures are holes in the drywall, a leak often shows up at a light or fan first. Keep that circuit off until someone has inspected it, since damp wiring may corrode or short out.

Insurance

Insurance and Roof Leak Water Damage

When a leak happens suddenly and by accident, for example a branch going through the roof or wind stripping shingles, many homeowners policies pay for the water damage inside. Insurers generally turn down claims tied to wear, age or lack of upkeep, and they may handle the roof repair on different terms than the inside damage. Photos and moisture readings from us go to the adjuster, though only your policy decides coverage, and your agent can explain it.
