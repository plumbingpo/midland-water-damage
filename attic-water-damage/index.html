Attic Water Damage Help in Midland

There are two ways an attic ends up wet. Rain gets in from above through worn shingles, failed flashing, or the seals around vents and chimneys. Moisture also rises from the house below and condenses on cold roof sheathing when the attic is not ventilated well. Either way, hardly anyone goes up there to look. In Midland, we inspect the attic, track down the source, and get it dry.

On the typical attic job, soaked insulation is the main issue. Fiberglass and cellulose both lose R-value when they get wet, so the home costs more to heat and cool. Worse, soaked insulation holds water against the ceiling drywall and the framing for weeks. Saturated material comes out, air movers and dehumidifiers dry the wood, and any mold on the sheathing or rafters gets treated.

Signs to Look For

  • Musty odor in upstairs closets or in the attic itself
  • Brown rings or stains on an upstairs ceiling, often worse after rain
  • Dark stains or streaks on the rafters and the underside of the roof deck
  • An upstairs ceiling where the paint has blistered or started to peel
  • Sheathing with fuzzy growth, black or white, concentrated toward the eaves
  • Flattened, matted or discolored insulation that stands out from what surrounds it
How We Fix It

Our Attic Water Damage Process

1

Inspect and Find the Source

To learn whether you have a leak, condensation or the two together, we go over the roof deck, the vents, the exhaust ducts and each flashing point. Thermal imaging and moisture meters show how far it reached.

2

Remove Wet Insulation

Insulation that is soaked or has mold gets bagged and carried out, giving the wood and the drywall below a chance to dry. Rooms under the attic are protected, and the route in and out stays contained.

3

Dry the Framing

With dehumidifiers and air movers, the sheathing, joists and rafters return to normal moisture levels. Visible mold is cleaned from the wood and an antimicrobial is applied.

4

Fix the Cause

You are shown the ventilation or roof problem to be corrected, and our findings can go to your roofer. New insulation is installed to bring back the R-value after the attic is staying dry.

If Nothing Is Done

The Damage Timeline

The first storm

Insulation Takes It

The insulation catches and absorbs whatever rain gets by the roofing. Because the rooms below show nothing, the leak is missed and no roof repair happens.

24 to 48 hours

Mold May Begin

Damp wood and paper facing in a warm, still attic are ideal for mold. It can start on the back side of the ceiling drywall and on the sheathing.

Weeks later

Ceiling Stains Appear

After repeated wetting, the insulation is saturated and water reaches the ceiling. In the room underneath, brown rings show up and the drywall may begin sagging.

After months

Rot Sets In

Sheathing delaminates and loses its hold on the roofing nails. Large areas of roof decking are covered in mold, while rafters that never dry begin to decay.

Behind the Surface

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Misrouted Exhaust Fans

A bathroom fan that vents into the attic instead of outdoors adds warm, moist air every day. On cold sheathing that air condenses, and the result is easy to mistake for a leaking roof.

Wet Wiring

Cable runs, junction boxes and recessed light housings are all found in attics. When they get wet there is a risk of shock and fire, so keep out until the power has been checked.

Ceiling Collapse

Soaked insulation carries a great deal of weight. The ceiling drywall under it weakens where it is fastened, and it can give way suddenly and bring the wet insulation along.

Leaks From Attic Equipment

Many attics hold an air handler, a drain pan and condensate lines. A clogged drain or an overflowing pan drips into the insulation for weeks, with no storm to warn you.
