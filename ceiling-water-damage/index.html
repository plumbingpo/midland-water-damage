Ceiling Water Damage Repair: What Midland Homeowners Should Know

When a brown circle finally appears above you, the leak has been at work for a while. Long before you see discoloration, the insulation is wet, moisture has followed the framing and a puddle sits on the hidden side of the panel. The leak may be a bathroom on the floor above, a pipe in the ceiling, an air conditioner drain line or the roof. Our team finds where it starts before any patching is done.

Painting over the stain does not fix it. Moisture left in the cavity brings the discoloration back and lets mold get going on the paper side in 24 to 48 hours. We open only what we need to, pull out wet insulation, dry the joists and remaining drywall, then patch, texture and paint so the repair blends in. A sagging ceiling is a different matter. Stay out from under it and call.

Signs to Look For

  • Water trails on the upper wall, starting at the ceiling corner
  • Patches of spotty discoloration that look like mold on the ceiling
  • A musty smell in one room with no visible source
  • Thin cracking in the ceiling finish centered on a wet looking area
  • Soft, bowed or sagging spots you can feel with a gentle push
  • Blistered, bubbling or flaking paint on the ceiling
Our Process

What Happens After You Call

1

Trace the Leak

The first job is locating the starting point, which involves meter readings, a thermal camera and a look at whatever is over the ceiling. The leak has to be stopped before repairs make sense.

2

Make It Safe

Power to wet fixtures is shut off and furniture below is covered or moved. Where water is trapped overhead, it gets drained under control into containers.

3

Remove and Dry Out

Damaged drywall and wet insulation come out. The joists and nearby ceiling are dried with air movers plus a dehumidifier, and antimicrobial goes on wherever the water was dirty.

4

Rebuild and Paint

After the meter shows normal levels, new insulation and drywall go up, joints are taped and finished, the texture is matched and a stain blocking primer goes on ahead of paint.

Behind the Surface

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Growth on the Hidden Side

Even when the ceiling feels dry to the touch, the batts and the unseen face of the drywall remain damp, giving mold a hidden place to spread.

Asbestos in Older Ceilings

Asbestos was used in certain older plasters and popcorn finishes. Testing comes first in such a home, ahead of any cutting, scraping or demolition of the damaged section.

A Ceiling That Lets Go

Once saturated, drywall weighs much more and no longer holds its screws well. Whatever sits under a swollen area is at risk if that section comes down.

The Leak Is Somewhere Else

The wet spot is often not below the leak, since water rides pipes, ducts and joists before it falls. Opening the ceiling only at the stain can miss wet material farther along.

If Nothing Is Done

The Damage Timeline

The first few hours

Hidden Travel Overhead

Moisture follows the joists and the insulation drinks it in. The drywall absorbs it from the back, so the room below may still look fine.

24 to 48 hours

Marks Start Showing

You see the first ring, along with blisters in the paint. Soaked insulation weighs on the drywall while mold can start on its paper face.

Days 3 through 7

The Ceiling Bows

Softened gypsum loses its hold on the screws. The ceiling bows between joists, seams open up and a musty odor reaches the room.

Past one week

Sections May Fall

Parts of the ceiling can come down along with the wet insulation resting on them. By now mold has settled into the space above, and the area needing repair has grown considerably.
